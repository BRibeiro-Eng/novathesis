In an ISO~50001-certified refinery, the energy performance of each process
unit is tracked by comparing daily consumption with an energy baseline that
is linear in throughput and re-estimated every year. In the crude
distillation unit studied, that number depended on three data systems and
manual spreadsheets, and the baseline had never been evaluated outside the
year it was fitted on. This work addresses both layers of that reliability.
First, a platform was built that brings the three sources into a single
dimensional model, updated daily and validated against the official reports
over 400 unit--vector--month cells, with the 94 initial discrepancies traced
to seven causes. Second, the production method was replicated exactly over
2020--2025 and evaluated under a protocol fixed before any result was seen.
For fuel gas, the baseline reduces next-year error by 30 to 40\% relative to
the training-year mean, but its slope falls by about 30\% over five years
and the alarm band loses coverage. For the three steam levels, throughput
carries no usable predictive information, and band coverage ranges from 0.38
to 0.997. None of the 32 linear candidates met the pre-registered acceptance
criterion. In a known-truth simulation, the choice of monitor mattered more
for detection than the choice of baseline. A scoping review of 331 eligible
publications found, among the 13 studies set in refining or petrochemicals,
none that combines measurement at the model boundary, a re-estimation rule,
a declared validity domain and out-of-sample testing. The thesis proposes
guidelines and a specification for the platform, not yet deployed, in which
the domain, performance and uncertainty of the baseline are visible to
whoever reads the alarm.

\bigskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} energy performance; energy baselines;
ISO~50006; oil refining; data reconciliation; out-of-sample evaluation
