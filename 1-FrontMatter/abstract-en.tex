In a certified energy management system, it is the energy baseline that
distinguishes normal consumption from a deviation worth investigating. The
standard defines it as the relation between consumption and the variables
that explain it; in practice, normalisation rests almost always on a single
variable --- processed throughput. In the crude distillation unit studied,
that relation is a straight line on throughput, replaced every year, and the
goodness of fit that justifies it is computed on the year that produced it.
Whether it is right in the year it is used, whether its parameters hold, and
whether the deviation it should flag survives the re-fit had never been
checked.

Evaluating the baseline required being able to trust the number it judges:
the three sources were brought into a single model, updated daily, that
reproduces the official reports. On it, the production method was replicated
exactly over six years of operation, outside the fitting year, in five
train/test pairs and with the criteria declared before any result was seen.

Of the four vectors evaluated, only fuel gas shows demonstrated predictive
gain --- 30 to 40\% less error than the training-year mean, in four of the
five pairs --- and it is there that the failure becomes visible: the slope
falls 30\% in five years, the annual re-estimation absorbs the drift, and
the band fails in the pairs that cross it. For the three steam levels
throughput brings no gain, so their bands pass or fail for reasons unrelated
to normalisation; in one of them, the band that should contain 95\% of days
contained between 38\% and 99.7\%. None of the 32 estimator--band
combinations evaluated met the declared criterion. Under the same
false-signal budget, in a known-truth simulation, replacing the level
monitor by a cumulative sum raised step detection by 32 to 49 percentage
points, against less than three when the linear baseline was replaced by an
additive model --- under a linear generator, which favours the linear one.

A scoping review of 331 publications shows that this way of certifying a
baseline is not particular to this unit: of the 140 models described, 13
report an out-of-sample result and 49 only the fit to their own training
data. No study set in refining or petrochemicals combines measurement at the
boundary, a re-estimation rule, a declared validity domain and out-of-sample
testing.

The consequence is not to change model: an annual baseline only serves as a
judgement once what it predicts outside its fitting year has been measured,
with the monitor specified and tested alongside it. What remains are those
conditions as requirements and a specification --- not deployed --- in which
the domain, performance and uncertainty of the baseline are visible to
whoever reads the alarm.
